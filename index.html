On-policy self-distillation

From endpoint reward to explicit intermediate supervision

Endpoint rewards indicate whether a final image is good, not how an intermediate denoising prediction should change. DiffusionOPSD closes this gap by synthesizing bounded self-teachers at sampled low-noise queries and distilling them as detached supervision.

01

Roll out EMA self-teacher

A frozen EMA self-teacher generates on-policy trajectories and supplies low-noise query states and stationary clean-output anchors.

02

Synthesize bounded self-teachers

Normalized local reward ascent and descent synthesize bounded positive and negative self-teachers inside a controlled clean-output radius.

03

Distill, refresh, repeat

The student policy distills detached self-teachers under a finite budget—admitting the exact single-branch variant DiffusionOPSD*—before EMA refreshes the self-teacher.

Paradigm & compute efficiency

In-trajectory self-distillation converges fastest and ranks top.

Across 20 target-reward settings on SD3.5-M and Z-Image-Turbo, DiffusionOPSD* and DiffusionOPSD reach 90% normalized held-out gain with 6.8× and 4.3× less compute than ReFL, outperforming trajectory-level baselines at 25% budget and final rank.

Diagnosable self-distillation

Separating self-teacher synthesis from student distillation

Stop-gradient detachment removes the decoder and reward model from student backward passes and lets us audit synthesis gain, realized student shifts, multi-reward transfer, and human preference independently.

Gradient dispersion −50.5%

01 · Training dynamics & retention

Fastest shared-scale progress with zero late-stage regression

Across all single-reward tasks on both backbones, DiffusionOPSD and DiffusionOPSD* retain medians of 98% and 100% of peak within-run gains—exceeding FlowGRPO by 13 percentage points and ReFL's lower tail by 20 points—while maintaining smooth 300-update multi-reward composite trajectories.

02 · Multi-reward & self-teacher ablations

Joint multi-reward transfer, step-distilled adaptation, and self-teacher variants

One shared SD3.5-M student retains up to 112.9% of specialist gains and boosts unguided GenEval accuracy by 117.9%. On Z-Image-Turbo, DiffusionOPSD and DiffusionOPSD* reach +97% mean normalized gain across all 10 objectives, while two-sided on-policy self-teachers outperform forward-noised (+3.9%), positive-only (+6.6%), and residual controls.

03 · Mechanism & efficiency audits

Iso-budget mechanism attribution, shift bounds, and rollout concordance

Single-step single-trajectory synthesis cuts measured training time by 30.5% while matching CLIPScore within 0.4%. Across four matched-budget stages, gradient normalization, stationary EMA anchoring, and adaptive residual scaling outperform coupled ReFL by up to 36.5% (22.5 SE), keeping 100% of shifts inside ρ = 0.10 and cutting off-query drift by 51.7% relative to DiffusionNFT.

04 · Robustness & human preference

Stable hyperparameter plateaus, CFG-free superiority, and blinded human wins

Held-out CLIPScore stays within one standard error across broad interior ranges of σq, ρ, and β, and CFG-free training avoids the up to 15.7% unguided drop of guided training. Across 2,400 single-reward and 1,500 multi-axis blinded ratings, annotators prefer DiffusionOPSD over every baseline—reaching 90% over DiffusionNFT—with consistent gains across all six DrawBench categories.

05 · Interpretation

Three measurements answer three different questions

  1. Synthesis gainDid local reward gradients synthesize a better clean-output self-teacher at the query?
  2. Realized gainDid the finite student distillation step preserve that improvement conservatively at the query and across regenerated rollouts?
  3. End-to-end qualityDid repeated synthesize–distill–refresh iterations improve held-out benchmarks, compositional accuracy, and human preference?

DiffusionOPSD exposes all three stages separately without retaining decoder or reward graphs during student backpropagation.
